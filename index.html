Hi, I'm Shanelle Dsouza, an 18-year-old digital and traditional artist currently in my second year of college. I create across mediums — painting, sketching, and writing poetry that often explores grief, love, and the quieter forms of heartbreak.

Draft 1: Love and Grief

November.
It's raining again. The kind of rain that doesn't rush, just lingers-
like the last light in a city that never really sleeps.

Yellow brick road.
I walked down the narrow street, hands stuffed into my pocket,
listening as the water collects in the cracks of the pavement.
The neon lights in fogged-up windows flicker weakly,
half-alive, half-tired of trying.

And then, I see him.

Standing beneath the dim glow of a streetlight,
leaning against the rusted railing of an old café.
A figure dressed in something dark, something soft.
His hair falls just enough to shadow his face,
but I know him.
Of course, I do.

He doesn't wave, doesn't call my name.
He only watches, as if waiting for me to decide.

I could keep walking. I could pretend not to notice.
But I stop.

"You again."

It's not a question.
Not a complaint.
Just something inevitable.

Grief smiles—not wide, not forced,
just enough to say, I never left.

"You always find me first," I murmur,
pulling my jacket tighter around my shoulders.

He tilts his head, eyes filled with something I can't name.
Not sorrow. Not pity. Just... knowing.

"Or maybe," he says, voice low, warm yet leaving a lingering cold,
"I was always walking beside you. You only notice when it rains."

I exhale sharply, a quiet laugh escaping before I can stop it.
Not because it's funny, but because it's true.

We start walking, side by side.
The silence between us is familiar, well-worn—
like an old book, read so many times the words don't need to be spoken aloud.

The street is mostly empty, save for the occasional headlights slicing through the mist.
The world keeps moving, indifferent to our quiet existence.

"I'm reeling in my brain yet again it always comes back to you,"
I confess, watching my reflection ripple in the puddles.

Grief hums, slow and thoughtful.
"You never let yourself forget. That's why I stay."

I swallow, the rain slipping down my skin like carving letters,
spelling out things I'm not ready to say.
"If I let go of you, what would be left?"

To let go of you means you'll never come back,
the love that remains is just a little fragment of you,
dried up and pressed like a flower to keep forever.

Grief doesn't answer immediately.
He looks up at the sky instead, watching as the clouds shift, restless.
Then, his voice, soft but certain—

"If you could let go of me, you wouldn't be you."

I don't know if that comforts me.
I don't know if I want it to.
Nevertheless.

We reach the end of the street, where the road meets the sea.
The waves crash gently against the shore, stretching endlessly into the horizon.

Grief walks ahead, stepping onto the sand,
barefoot, unbothered by the cold,
the warmth he held washed away by the salty waves.

I hesitate, watching him.
The way he stood with his hands in his pockets,
gazing at the ocean as if it holds every answer he's never said aloud.

"Do you ever get tired?" I ask, finally stepping beside him.
"Of being here? Of following me?"

Grief turns to me, studying my face,
like he's memorizing the way I look tonight—
the way I look every time I ask this question.

"Do you?" he asked back.

I don't answer. I don't need to.

The waves creep closer, the tide rising slowly.
The street behind us fades into nothing but distant lights.

"Grief and love walk this world hand in hand—"
I say, more to myself than to him.
"One would not exist without the other, the same way you and I are bound by something neither of us dare to name."

Grief exhales, tilting his head toward the sea.
"And yet, you still wish I would leave."

I part my lips, ready to agree, ready to say yes,
but something in me hesitates.
Something in me aches at the thought of watching him disappear.

I look at him, as my heart beats a little softer—
the way his presence has become a shadow stitched into my own.
The way his voice carries an understanding no one else ever could.

"No," I whisper.

Grief blinks, caught off guard, and I think this is the first time
I've ever surprised him.

"Undeniably i still got love for you," I admit, voice shaking.
"A part of me always will."

Grief stares at me for a long moment.
And then, he smiles—something small, something knowing.

"I know."

The tide curls around my ankles, and for once,
I don't move away.

And neither does he.

The love i once anchored was now set free.

Draft 2: To Gracey

This house is too quiet.

The kind of quiet that seeps into your bones,
settles into the walls,
makes a home out of the spaces you once filled.

I wake up before the sun again,
but there is no warmth beside me.
Just the cold stretch of sheets, undisturbed.

Even after all this time,
I still turn to tell you things.

I catch myself in the mirror sometimes.
the lines on my face deeper than they were before,
my hands more hesitant as they reach
for a teacup that once had a pair.

Dusty.
The polaroids on the dresser have started to drain off their colour.
Your smile...once so bright,
now barely holding onto the ink.

I should put them away.
Maybe even shred them away.

Why are you so pretty?

My hands refuse to let you go,
as if losing you in memory
would be the last betrayal.

"Won't you stay?"

I whisper it into the air,
into the empty seat at the table,
into the space beside me
where your pillow still rests untouched.

Love doesn't vanish.
It lingers in the places time cannot touch,
in the chair you always sat in,
in the old songs that still hum through the radio,
in the way the house still smells like your favorite flowers,
even though no fresh ones have been placed in years.

Gone were the warm breeze ruffling your hair,
and so were you, my darling, my Gracey.

I'll live only to die in you,
while you die but continue to live in me.

For as long as I am,
you'll be the happiest part of me,
and I'll be the saddest part of you.

The world tells me to move on.
To keep going, to let go,
but what do they know?

"Where love was, love will always be."

And where you were,
I will always remain.

They say everything has a time limit.
That even love has an expiration date.
But I think they are wrong.

Because even now, long after the clocks have stopped for you,
you are still my sun.
My Gracey.

And I,
I am simply the one left behind,
standing in the light you left behind,
waiting.

with love,
Ignatius

(For what greater punishment is there than life,
when the one you lived for is gone?)

Draft 3: Last Letter to your Lover

I write to you one last time.

With fingers worn by time, calloused
with ink that seeps through parchment
like something desperate to be remembered.
My hands tremble as they carve your name,
each stroke mirroring the lines on my face.

I write to you with a wilted mind that strives to recall your face.
Still, the weight of your absence unsettles me,
like an old wound that refuses to close.

Love was never meant to be measured,
but I knew from the start
I would always want more than what you could give.

Love wasn't enough.
You could only offer a fraction of what i desired.

It wasn't your fault.
It wasn't mine either.
It was simply the way our hearts were built
one always overflowing,
the other struggling to hold enough.

The more beautiful my thoughts of you,
the more my chest ached under their weight.

I could fill volumes with the memory of us,
but you remain only a story now,
a picture I could trace with my eyes closed.

I taught myself to imagine a life without you,
to soften the sharp edges of your absence.
Like I always knew you would leave.

Or perhaps I willed it into existence.

I could never tear myself away from you entirely.

Eight letters, I love you.
I say them to another,
but a part of me is still whispering them to you.
When I look into the eyes of another,
I still search for your auburn gaze.
I find pieces of you in strangers,
a quiet proof that you were once real.

And yet, I write to you now
with a heart quieter than before,
a love worn thin by time.

This is my farewell
not bitter, not sweet,
but something softer,
like a wave retreating from the shore.

If these words ever find you,
let them tell you what I cannot
that I have loved you in every way I knew how,
and that even now,
somewhere beneath my ribs,
I still do.

(And still, I write
even as my fingers grow numb,
as ink fades to something closer to blood.)

Draft 4: Music Box

The world spins in slow, deliberate turns.

A gentle waltz, a song long set in motion
one that neither of us remembers starting.

The air is thick with a melody we have danced to before,
though neither of us know the name of the song.

Inside the music box, time is patient.
We move as we always have,
one step forward, two steps back,
in a way our hands graze but never fully hold.
One look into your eyes is to see more in myself
than I ever dared to before.

I do not ask if you will stay.
The lid will close eventually,
the melody will slow,
and I will be left to spin alone.

Still, I let my fingers brush yours,
if only to know what it feels like.
If only to carve the shape of you into memory.

For now, love is this.
Love is the way our shadows intertwine,
the way our bodies understand what our hearts refuse to name.

I have rewritten the definition of love
a thousand times over,
searching for a version that does not ache.

For now, love is you.

The music box hums.
The world turns again.

"Lean on me," I whisper,
though I do not know if you hear it.

Just ten seconds
let me hold you in my gaze,
let me press you into this moment
so that when the song ends,
I will still have something left of you.

And when the final note lingers in the air
when the lid closes and silence settles in
will your eyes still wander in search of me?

Will you miss me before you go?

Or will I be nothing more
than a song left unfinished,
a figure spinning in the dark,
waiting for the music to start again?

Draft 5: The Almosts

"You're staring again."

"You always notice."

"Because I always look for you."

The words slip between us like smoke, curling at the edges, dissipating before they can settle. A breath apart, a universe between. We've always been like this—threading the needle between wanting and leaving, holding and letting go. A battle we never win.

"People like us don't end up together," he murmurs, his fingers ghosting the rim of his glass, as if drawing circles around something inevitable.

"People like us?"

"The almosts." His gaze meets mine, heavy, unreadable. "The ones who step too close to the fire but never burn."

The weight of it sits between us, thick and unrelenting. The city hums outside, but in this space, it is only us—us and the ache that never settles, only lingers.

"And what do we do then?" I ask, voice quieter now.

He tilts his head, watching me as if memorising the shape of my hesitation. "We pretend."

"And if I stop pretending?"

A pause. His breath catches, a flicker of something breaking through the cracks. A hand twitches, almost reaching, almost crossing that fragile line.

He exhales, slow, measured, as if weighing the weight of his own words before letting them go. "Then we unravel."

A pause—thick, suffocating. He looks at me like he already knows what I'll say, like he's read it in the silence between us, in the spaces where words should've been. And then, I lean back, just slightly, just enough to let the distance settle again.

"Then let us be ruins." I tilt my head, the corners of my lips curving—something between a smile and surrender.

And in the quiet that follows, I wonder if this is what heartbreak feels like—
not a sharp, sudden thing, but a slow unraveling,
a war never fought,
a love never given the chance to lose.

Draft 6: Violence Unstitched

Sometimes, I fear I will become what I hate.
That his voice will slip into mine,
sharp and heavy, cutting without a blade.
That my hands will learn the language of fists,
speaking in violence and my words twist.

I have spent my life watching him—
studying every storm, every slammed door,
every silence that followed like an aftershock.

I grew up watching love leave bruises.
Not always on skin, but in the way my mother's hands shook when she reached for the salt at dinner.
In the way she spoke—measured, careful, rehearsed.
I learned that love meant walking on eggshells,
that devotion looked a lot like endurance.

I swore I'd be different.
I swore I'd unlearn the rage stitched into my name.

But what if it's in my blood?
What if anger is the only inheritance he left me?
What if love, to me, will always feel like a battlefield,
where quiet feels like a warning and kindness feels like a lie?

I do not want to be him.
I do not want to love like a clenched jaw,
like a hand gripping too tight,
like something that leaves bruises even when it swears it didn't mean to.

I want love to feel like open arms,
like quiet that doesn't threaten to break,
like warmth that doesn't burn.

But I don't know what to do with it.

A voice without anger sounds empty.
A touch without force feels insincere.
A love without chaos feels unreal.

I try to tell myself that love shouldn't hurt,
but if it doesn't leave a scar, did it ever really touch me?

Draft 7: Speak Now or Forever Hold Your Peace

Let me miss him now, in the rawness of midnight,
when the air is thick with ghosts and my heart still aches sweetly.
Let me miss him with reckless abandon,
in the songs that gut me,
in the spaces where his laughter used to echo.
Let me tear myself apart for him now, if I must—
but God, don't let me miss him in a wedding dress.

Not when the veil is drawn and my hands are
wrapped around another's,
when love should taste like forever but feels like a requiem.
Not when I stand before another man who loves me right,
who gives me everything except the fire i once burned for.

Don't let me look in the congregation of a thousand,
searching for a face that should not be in there,
don't let my gaze flicker, desperate and wanting.
Don't let me wonder if he ever thought of me,
if he ever ran, breathless, through the streets to stop me—
only twenty-five minutes too late.

Don't let my vows tremble with regret.
Don't let his name be the ghost beneath my breath.

Let me burn for him now, so I do not smoulder in silk and lace.
Let me grieve him now, so I do not weep at the altar.
Let me love him now

Draft 8: Stay, Even in Decay

You don't even know how to leave.
You slipped out the door but forgot your breath on my window.
Your voice still bruises the walls.
Your absence lingers like secondhand smoke—
caught in my throat,
burning like a cigarette, just never enough to kill me.

You left yourself to me.
In fingerprints on cold cups,
in the bedsheets you folded wrong,
in that one sock I keep finding in places it doesn't belong.
Infact you didn't leave.
You bled.
All over my hands.
All over my mouth.
I still taste you in the prayers i say.

And maybe you never meant to stay,
but your ghost made a home in the pit of my stomach,
nestled between the ache.

And i think
You'll make the prettiest flowers
when your body rots into the earth.
When i plant you with bare hands.
Nails digging past bone and memory,
watering you with the ache you left in my teeth—
where I bit down words like
please
stay

Maybe you'll bloom into something beautiful.
Maybe I'll clip your petals and press them in a book,
But the words I wrote around you grew damp-
mold creeping over the margins
because the flowers never fully dried.
You still weep between the pages.
You rot slowly, lovingly,
between everything I tried to preserve.

You're the rot and the bloom.
You're the seed and my sickness.

Maybe I'll keep you there.
Where even dead feel alive.
In pages people dare not look.

Draft 9: A Eulogy for the Living

"You're growing up so fast,"
they say, again—
soft, like a passing breeze
meant not to stay,
but to stir something inside me.

I laugh
because they do,
because that's what we do
when we don't know
how to say goodbye
in advance.

But tonight,
it echoes longer.

"Do you mean it?" I ask.

They don't answer right away.
Just glance at each other,
a silent agreement folded
into the wrinkles around their eyes.

"You were just this tall,"
Dad gestures with his hand.
"And now—"
he doesn't finish.

Mom touches my hair like it's slipping away.
"We're watching you bloom," she says,
but her voice holds the tremble
of petals falling.

"Are you scared?" I whisper.

"Not of leaving," she says.
"Only of not being there
when you need someone to stay."

Dad exhales,
and I swear
the whole room sinks with it.

"We won't be in all your stories,"
he says,
"but we'll be the beginning
of every one."

I want to scream,
I want time to stop,
I want this joke to go back to being a joke.
But I only nod,
because I don't know how to hold
something I know I will lose.

"You're growing up so fast,"
they say again,
and this time
I hear it for what it is—

not pride,
but preparation.
Not a joke,
but a farewell
waiting its turn.

And I don't know how to stop
the ache that sits in the room after that.
So I reach for their hands—
warm, worn,
so painfully alive.

Something inside me starts writing
a eulogy
for a moment
that hasn't ended yet.

Draft 10: Soda Theory

"I thought you'd hate it if I drank your Soda,"
you said.
And I laughed,
because I thought you hated me cause you didn't drink it.

And I,
thinking your silence meant distance,
let the fizz go flat in my throat
before I ever learned
you were only being gentle
in the way I never asked for.

I held out the bottle,
I watched you hesitate,
the same boy who never stops talking, quiet now
you looked at it
like it meant something more—
and maybe it did.
Maybe it was a truce.
Maybe it was a test.

And maybe we both failed.

You remembered I don't like sharing.
I remembered every time I wished you'd forget.

I wonder how many more things we'll misunderstand
in the name of love.
How strange it is—
that love sometimes sounds like
"I didn't drink it because I thought of you,"
and still feels like
"I didn't drink it because I didn't want you."

We speak in hesitations,
and hear in heartbreaks.

You didn't drink it anyway.
And that was your way of saying you care.

But I only tasted the silence.

But maybe next time,
when I pass you the bottle,
you'll drink
not to quench thirst—
but to tell me you're still here.

And maybe next time,
I'll understand what you meant all along.
That you loved me all along.

(p.s. As i love you too)

Draft 11: A Conversation

It's March again.
And I'm laughing at the same jokes, just in a different body.
He says things we said two years ago,
only this time, I'm the one holding the cigarette like a promise I won't keep.
Counting the cracks in the pavement like regrets i'm starting to feel.

I told you—
love always echoes before it fades.
You just never listened to how loud your silence was.

He talks about how he wants to be a better father than the one he got.
Same words, same tremble in the voice.
Only now it's me nodding, saying things I don't believe in anymore.

He looks at me with eyes that plead for a future.
Do you remember?
That used to be me.
I was so soft, I bled at every edge.

And now?
Now you're the one holding the knife by the blade.
Now you're me.
And he's you.
Is it easier to shatter someone when you already know how the pieces fall?

I don't mean to.
God, I don't.
But everything he says pulls a memory from my throat.
And I think I'm wearing your skin.
I look in the mirror and see myself stalling the way you did.
I know how it ends.
And I know he won't survive it like I did.

So you're me now.
And he's the you I held onto until my fingers turned blue.
Do you finally understand what I was trying to hold together?

You're haunted now.
Welcome.
Did you think ghosts were just stories or soft whispers in dreams?
No—
they wear your face when you're about to break someone the way we broke each other.

And it's terrifying.
Because I like him..
He holds my hand like I held yours—
hopeful.
He smiles like I used to—
History doesn't repeat—it performs.
And I'm the actor reading lines from a script I pretended not to write.

If you knew how it ends, why open the curtain again?

Because hope is the cruelest kind of nostalgia.
Because maybe if I play your part right,
he won't have to become me.

So you stay?

I think I just wanted to be forgiven without being punished.
But time—
time remembers.
And this time, I'm the lesson.

Draft 12: Self Sabotage

all the whites came out clean except one—
my favourite one.
white, black rims,
a bow stitched dead-center like a target.

no red cloth. no mistake.
just itself,
bleeding into itself.

the black rims turned the water pink—
a quiet kind of violence.
self-made.
unprovoked.

I stood there, holding it,
the way you hold news you can't return.

and i tried to fix it.
i scrubbed, rinsed,
begged the fibers to forget.

this time,
the stains let go.

but next time—
what then?

should i wash it alone,
cold water, inside out,
like loving something you know might hurt itself again?

should i guard it from its own edges?
as if care could outwit what's stitched into the seams?

it still fits.
but i wear it less.

Draft 13: Me Before You

you forgave me for things i would've set fire to you for.
that's not grace, that's desperation in a nice suit.

i trace the outlines of old sins and paint them on your chest
just to see if you'll flinch—
spoiler: you never do.

you keep saying i'm the best thing that's ever happened to you, while i stay up at night wondering if i'm even real in your hands
or just some ghost wearing kindness like a mask.

you think i'm gentle—
but i've been rehearsing your heartbreak since day one.
picking at the thread, watching how far it unravels before you call it love again.

i want to want you the way you want me—
but some nights, i still whisper someone else's name in the back of my head and hate myself less than i should for it.

i'm not whole in your arms.
i just hold still long enough for you to believe i am and i let you.
because you think my silence means safety
when really, it's where i bury all the truths that would shatter you.

the sick part is—
you make me feel safe too.
safe enough to test just how far i can twist the knife before you bleed.

you think you're hard to love,
but god—
you made it so easy for me to take advantage of your grace.
you gave it freely and i pocketed it like stolen sugar from someone else's table.

and when you asked, half-breaking,
"will i always have you?"

i said yes.
hesitantly. carefully.
the same way i said it to someone else once—
the same someone i still think about when you fall asleep, holding my hand like a lifeline that i never deserved.

Draft 14: Sparks

what kind of coward loves like this—
spoon-feeding warmth to one while shivering for another?

i sit across from a boy who doesn't belong to me
and still — i want to give him everything i don't have.
he says something kind, and it splits me in half.
god, i want to love him. i almost do.

he feels like spring after ten winters.
like lungs remembering how to breathe.
he talks and i bloom.
not for the one who holds me but for the one who doesn't even know he could.

i'd stay up 'til the sky forgets to be black just to hear him exist,

Draft 15: Survivor's Guilt

I bite down on joy until my lips bleed,
spit it out before you see me smiling.

Your grief is a house on fire,
and I sit outside with clean clothes,
guilty for not burning.

And I hate it.
I hate the way my ease makes a mockery of your ache,
the way my peace sounds louder when your silence is raw.

My comfort feels obscene beside you.
Every smile is a crime scene,
every breath a betrayal.

But secretly—
I wonder if my love is useless,
if my sacrifice is empty,
if I am just another ghost haunting your suffering
instead of leading you out.

Call it love—
but it feels like suicide in installments,
dying a little each day
just to prove I will not abandon you.
