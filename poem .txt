Draft1. Love and Grief

November.
It's raining again. The kind of rain that doesn't rush, just lingers-
like the last light in a city that never really sleeps.

Yellow brick road. 
I walked down the narrow street, hands stuffed into my pocket, 
listening as the water collects in the cracks of the pavement.
The neon lights in fogged-up windows flicker weakly,
half-alive, half-tired of trying.

And then, I see him.

Standing beneath the dim glow of a streetlight,
leaning against the rusted railing of an old café.
A figure dressed in something dark, something soft.
His hair falls just enough to shadow his face,
but I know him.
Of course, I do.

He doesn't wave, doesn't call my name.
He only watches, as if waiting for me to decide.

I could keep walking. I could pretend not to notice.
But I stop.

"You again."

It's not a question.
Not a complaint.
Just something inevitable.

Grief smiles—not wide, not forced,
just enough to say, I never left.

"You always find me first," I murmur,
pulling my jacket tighter around my shoulders.

He tilts his head, eyes filled with something I can't name.
Not sorrow. Not pity. Just... knowing.

"Or maybe," he says, voice low, warm yet leaving a lingering cold,
"I was always walking beside you. You only notice when it rains."

I exhale sharply, a quiet laugh escaping before I can stop it.
Not because it's funny, but because it's true.

We start walking, side by side.
The silence between us is familiar, well-worn—
like an old book, read so many times the words don't need to be spoken aloud.

The street is mostly empty, save for the occasional headlights slicing through the mist.
The world keeps moving, indifferent to our quiet existence.

"I'm reeling in my brain yet again it always comes back to you,"
I confess, watching my reflection ripple in the puddles.

Grief hums, slow and thoughtful.
"You never let yourself forget. That's why I stay."

I swallow, the rain slipping down my skin like carving letters,
spelling out things I'm not ready to say.
"If I let go of you, what would be left?"

To let go of you means you'll never come back,
the love that remains is just a little fragment of you, 
dried up and pressed like a flower to keep forever.

Grief doesn't answer immediately.
He looks up at the sky instead, watching as the clouds shift, restless.
Then, his voice, soft but certain—

"If you could let go of me, you wouldn't be you."

I don't know if that comforts me.
I don't know if I want it to.
Nevertheless.

We reach the end of the street, where the road meets the sea.
The waves crash gently against the shore, stretching endlessly into the horizon.

Grief walks ahead, stepping onto the sand,
barefoot, unbothered by the cold, 
the warmth he held washed away by the salty waves.

I hesitate, watching him.
The way he stood  with his hands in his pockets,
gazing at the ocean as if it holds every answer he's never said aloud.

"Do you ever get tired?" I ask, finally stepping beside him.
"Of being here? Of following me?"

Grief turns to me, studying my face,
like he's memorizing the way I look tonight—
the way I look every time I ask this question.

"Do you?" he asked back.

I don't answer. I don't need to.

The waves creep closer, the tide rising slowly.
The street behind us fades into nothing but distant lights.

"Grief and love walk this world hand in hand—"
I say, more to myself than to him.
"One would not exist without the other, the same way you and I are bound by something neither of us dare to name."

Grief exhales, tilting his head toward the sea.
"And yet, you still wish I would leave."

I part my lips, ready to agree, ready to say yes,
but something in me hesitates.
Something in me aches at the thought of watching him disappear.

I look at him, as my heart beats a little softer—
the way his presence has become a shadow stitched into my own.
The way his voice carries an understanding no one else ever could.

"No," I whisper.

Grief blinks, caught off guard, and I think this is the first time
I've ever surprised him.

"Undeniably i still got love for you," I admit, voice shaking.
"A part of me always will."

Grief stares at me for a long moment.
And then, he smiles—something small, something knowing.

"I know."

The tide curls around my ankles, and for once,
I don't move away.

And neither does he.

The love i once anchored was now set free.


Draft2. To Gracey

This house is too quiet.

The kind of quiet that seeps into your bones,
settles into the walls,
makes a home out of the spaces you once filled.

I wake up before the sun again,
but there is no warmth beside me.
Just the cold stretch of sheets, undisturbed.

Even after all this time,
I still turn to tell you things.

I catch myself in the mirror sometimes.
the lines on my face deeper than they were before,
my hands more hesitant as they reach
for a teacup that once had a pair.

Dusty.
The polaroids on the dresser have started to drain off their colour.
Your smile...once so bright,
now barely holding onto the ink.

I should put them away.
Maybe even shred them away.

Why are you so pretty? 

My hands refuse to let you go,
as if losing you in memory
would be the last betrayal.

"Won't you stay?"

I whisper it into the air,
into the empty seat at the table, 
into the space beside me
where your pillow still rests untouched.

Love doesn't vanish.
It lingers in the places time cannot touch,
in the chair you always sat in,
in the old songs that still hum through the radio,
in the way the house still smells like your favorite flowers,
even though no fresh ones have been placed in years.

Gone were the warm breeze ruffling your hair,
and so were you, my darling, my Gracey.

I'll live only to die in you,
while you die but continue to live in me.

For as long as I am,
you'll be the happiest part of me,
and I'll be the saddest part of you.

The world tells me to move on.
To keep going, to let go,
but what do they know?

"Where love was, love will always be."

And where you were,
I will always remain.

They say everything has a time limit.
That even love has an expiration date.
But I think they are wrong.

Because even now, long after the clocks have stopped for you,
you are still my sun.
My Gracey.

And I,
I am simply the one left behind,
standing in the light you left behind,
waiting.

with love,
Ignatius




(For what greater punishment is there than life,
when the one you lived for is gone?)



Draft3. Last Letter to your Lover


I write to you one last time.

With fingers worn by time, calloused
with ink that seeps through parchment
like something desperate to be remembered.
My hands tremble as they carve your name,
each stroke mirroring the lines on my face.

I write to you with a wilted mind that strives to recall your face.
Still, the weight of your absence unsettles me,
like an old wound that refuses to close.

Love was never meant to be measured,
but I knew from the start
I would always want more than what you could give.

Love wasn't enough.
You could only offer a fraction of what i desired.

It wasn't your fault.
It wasn't mine either.
It was simply the way our hearts were built
one always overflowing,
the other struggling to hold enough.

The more beautiful my thoughts of you,
the more my chest ached under their weight.

I could fill volumes with the memory of us,
but you remain only a story now,
a picture I could trace with my eyes closed.

I taught myself to imagine a life without you,
to soften the sharp edges of your absence.
Like I always knew you would leave.

Or perhaps I willed it into existence.

I could never tear myself away from you entirely.

Eight letters, I love you.
I say them to another,
but a part of me is still whispering them to you.
When I look into the eyes of another,
I still search for your auburn gaze.
I find pieces of you in strangers,
a quiet proof that you were once real.

And yet, I write to you now
with a heart quieter than before,
a love worn thin by time.

This is my farewell
not bitter, not sweet,
but something softer,
like a wave retreating from the shore.

If these words ever find you,
let them tell you what I cannot
that I have loved you in every way I knew how,
and that even now,
somewhere beneath my ribs,
I still do.


(And still, I write
even as my fingers grow numb,
as ink fades to something closer to blood.)


Draft4. Music Box

The world spins in slow, deliberate turns.

A gentle waltz, a song long set in motion
one that neither of us remembers starting.

The air is thick with a melody we have danced to before,
though neither of us know the name of the song.

Inside the music box, time is patient.
We move as we always have,
one step forward, two steps back,
in a way our hands graze but never fully hold.
One look into your eyes is to see more in myself
than I ever dared to before.

I do not ask if you will stay.
The lid will close eventually,
the melody will slow,
and I will be left to spin alone.

Still, I let my fingers brush yours,
if only to know what it feels like.
If only to carve the shape of you into memory.

For now, love is this.
Love is the way our shadows intertwine,
the way our bodies understand what our hearts refuse to name.

I have rewritten the definition of love
a thousand times over,
searching for a version that does not ache.

For now, love is you.

The music box hums.
The world turns again.

"Lean on me," I whisper,
though I do not know if you hear it.

Just ten seconds
let me hold you in my gaze,
let me press you into this moment
so that when the song ends,
I will still have something left of you.

And when the final note lingers in the air
when the lid closes and silence settles in
will your eyes still wander in search of me?

Will you miss me before you go?

Or will I be nothing more
than a song left unfinished,
a figure spinning in the dark,
waiting for the music to start again?
